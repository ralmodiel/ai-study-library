Build Side Projects With Real Users to Stand Out to Employers

Summary

Bashiri Smith builds on advice from an unnamed tech influencer. Side projects help you get hired, but only if they have "signal": real users, production-level problems, impressive engineering, and something people want to share. Projects that nobody uses still teach you, but they won't make you stand out. Even a few real users let you tell a stronger interview story: you built it, launched it, saw what broke, and improved it.

Key points

  • Building many small apps that nobody uses or sees mostly helps you learn. It doesn't make you stand out to employers.
  • Projects that get attention have signal: real users, production-level problems, technically impressive engineering, and enough interest that people share them.
  • You don't need millions of users. A small number of real users gives you production-level experience.
  • Interview story to aim for: 'I built it, launched it, people used it, here's what broke, and here's how I improved it.' That counts as real engineering experience.
  • Quoted advice: sharpen your skills with lots of programming, go deep on the area you want to work in, and build small side projects you can show off.
  • Don't rely too heavily on vibe coding. You want people who read your code to think it's good code.
  • Put projects on the internet as clickable things people can try. A small viral side project can get you noticed.

Try this

  • Build side projects that real people actually use, not just practice apps.
  • Launch your project publicly and get at least a small number of real users.
  • Write code yourself rather than vibe coding heavily, so the code holds up when people read it.
  • Make your projects clickable and shareable online so they can spread.
  • Keep track of what broke in production and how you fixed it, so you can talk about it in interviews.
  • Watch the creator's full project blueprint video (comment 'impressive' or use the caption link).
  • A small, shareable side project with real users that deals with production-level problems.
