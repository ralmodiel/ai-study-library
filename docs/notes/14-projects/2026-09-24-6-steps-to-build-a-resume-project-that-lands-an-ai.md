# 6 Steps to Build a Resume Project That Lands an AI Engineering Job

## Summary

Bashiri Smith lays out a six-step framework for building portfolio projects that get you hired as an engineer in 2026. You start from the companies you want to work for, research the problems they are hiring to solve, and pick one real problem to study in depth. Then you design and build a focused solution with deliberate choices about model, infrastructure, reliability, cost and evaluation, and launch it publicly with a clean repo, a demo and a write-up.

## Key points

- Step 1, Targeting: rank what you want (pay, impact, company brand, stability, culture, personal mission). Choose a target profile (industry, company stage, relationship to AI), list 10–100 real companies, then filter to the ones where your background fits.
- Step 2, Market research: track jobs in Google Sheets or Excel. Add columns for basic info, common job-description themes, news articles, open-source work or engineering blogs, and tech stack.
- Build a 'silent network': connect on LinkedIn with people on your target teams before you apply.
- Step 3, Problem selection: check that the problem is real and specific, shared across many target companies, buildable in 1–2 months, a way to show domain depth, and interesting to you.
- Step 4, Research camp: spend 1–2 weeks learning what the problem is, why it exists and how to solve it. Ask your LinkedIn connections in-depth questions, then write a research brief.
- Step 5, Design and build: map the system and decide whether you need AI at all. Don't force RAG or agents into it. If you do use AI, focus on 1–2 core AI patterns, be ready to justify your model and infrastructure choices, and plan for design, reliability, cost and evaluation.
- Step 6, Launch: ship a clean repo, an understandable README and a live demo video. Write a Medium or blog post about the story and your architecture decisions, post on LinkedIn, Reddit and other communities, and use the project in your CV, emails and follow-ups.

## Try this

- [ ] Rank your job priorities: compensation, impact, brand, stability, culture and mission.
- [ ] Define a target profile (industry, company stage, relationship to AI) and list 10–100 real companies.
- [ ] Filter that list to the companies where your experience and background fit.
- [ ] Set up a job tracker in Google Sheets or Excel with columns for job-description themes, news, open-source work or engineering blogs, and tech stack.
- [ ] Connect on LinkedIn with people on your target teams before you apply.
- [ ] Score candidate problems: real and specific, shared across companies, buildable in 1–2 months, shows domain depth, interests you.
- [ ] Do a 1–2 week research camp, interview your connections, and write a research brief.
- [ ] Map the system and decide whether AI is needed; if it is, limit it to 1–2 core AI patterns.
- [ ] Document your reasons for the model and infrastructure, and plan for reliability, cost and evaluation.
- [ ] Launch with a clean repo, a clear README, a live demo video and a blog post, then share it on LinkedIn, Reddit, and in your CV and follow-ups.
- [ ] Build a 1–2 month project that solves one real problem shared across many of your target companies, chosen from their job postings, engineering blogs and conversations with their engineers.
