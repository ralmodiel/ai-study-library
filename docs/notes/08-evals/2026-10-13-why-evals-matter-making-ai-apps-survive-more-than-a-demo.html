Why Evals Matter: Making AI Apps Survive More Than a Demo

Summary

Bashiri Smith argues that because LLM outputs are non-deterministic, getting one good answer doesn't prove your AI system is reliable. Evaluation is the key skill for engineers moving into AI roles. He quotes an unnamed AI expert who says hiring may come down to building a big project that holds up when someone tries to break it with tools like Codex. He lists five areas to evaluate (retrieval quality, groundedness, task success, guardrails, cost/latency) and shares his free AI evaluation guide.

Key points

  • LLMs are non-deterministic: the same input can give different outputs, so one good answer doesn't prove reliability.
  • Test your system with messy questions, irrelevant context and adversarial instructions meant to break it, not just the happy path.
  • Retrieval quality: did the RAG system actually find the information needed to answer?
  • Groundedness: is the answer supported by the retrieved information?
  • Task success: did the agent actually complete the job?
  • Guardrails: how does the app handle attempts to manipulate it (e.g. prompt injection)?
  • Cost and latency: measure cost per successful request and how long each request takes.
  • Evals help you find failures, measure improvements and catch regressions before users do. The creator says many engineers moving into AI roles have weak eval skills, and many job postings require them.
