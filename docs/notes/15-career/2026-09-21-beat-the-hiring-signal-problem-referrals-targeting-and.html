Beat the Hiring "Signal Problem": Referrals, Targeting, and Volume

Summary

Hiring managers get thousands of applications, and the resumes all look the same, so being qualified isn't enough. You also need to get seen. Bashiri Smith gives three ways to stand out: build a referral network by offering value and proof of your skills, apply only to roles where you're a strong fit, and keep sending lots of applications while you improve your resume and projects.

Key points

  • The CEO of a major ATS (applicant tracking system) and hiring platform calls this a 'signal problem': with a thousand similar resumes, a recruiter can't tell who is qualified, who is interested and who auto-applied.
  • The creator says landing a tech job today is '90% about just being seen.'
  • Strategy 1, referrals: email people at companies you want to join and offer them value or proof of your skills. Also offer value to people already in your network: peers, coworkers, professors, bosses, businesses and LinkedIn connections.
  • Strategy 2, targeting: don't send your resume to every company. Apply where you're actually qualified and well placed, so your resume looks like the perfect fit when it's seen.
  • Strategy 3, volume and time: this works even if you have no unique experience or network, as long as you keep updating your resume and building projects.
  • The creator says he has never seen anyone with a good resume, good projects and decent experience who sends 100–500 applications a month fail to land a job eventually.
  • Sending the same generic resume to every opening isn't a strategy.

Try this

  • Email people at the companies you want to work for and offer them value or proof of your skills.
  • Offer value to people already in your network (peers, coworkers, professors, bosses, businesses, LinkedIn connections) so you can get referrals.
  • Apply only to roles where you're qualified and well placed instead of sending your resume everywhere.
  • Keep improving your resume and building projects while sending a high volume of applications (100–500 a month).
