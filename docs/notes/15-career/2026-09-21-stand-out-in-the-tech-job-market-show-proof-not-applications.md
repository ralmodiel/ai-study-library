# Stand Out in the Tech Job Market: Show Proof, Not Applications

Bashiri Smith · Facebook reel · 2026-09-21 · 1:12 · 6,419 views · [Open on Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/reel/2261983224625815)

**Topics:** Resume, Job Search & Interviews, Portfolio Projects, Industry Trends & Job Market · **Level:** beginner

## Summary

Bashiri Smith argues that the people struggling most to get hired in tech are "boring" candidates: people with average degrees or jobs who never built anything significant. Getting hired is now a fight to be noticed, and you win it with visible proof that you've built things and solved real problems. Mass-applying, complaining, and building the same project as everyone else won't work. Showing domain knowledge and high agency will.

## Key points

- Hiring is hardest for candidates with nothing distinctive: an average CS degree or a job where they didn't build anything significant.
- The old path no longer works: send a resume, get an interview, pass it, get the job.
- Getting hired now means being visible, and the way to be visible is proof that you built or achieved something significant for a company, yourself, or someone else.
- Ways to stand out: build interesting projects, post about them consistently, start a startup or freelance business, or build things for friends.
- What doesn't work: sending thousands of applications, complaining about the job market, building the same project as everyone else, or chasing whatever is trending on X (e.g. 'harness engineering').
- Companies want proof that you can solve their problems. Show domain knowledge and high agency: find a problem and build a solution.
- The creator says he has seen hundreds of people he didn't expect to get hired land jobs in the past year.

## Try this

- [ ] Stop mass-applying with a generic resume. Focus on being visible instead.
- [ ] Build original projects rather than the same project everyone else builds.
- [ ] Post about what you build consistently.
- [ ] Consider starting a startup or a freelance business, or building tools for friends.
- [ ] Show domain knowledge in a specific problem area.
- [ ] Show high agency: find a real problem on your own and build a solution to it.
- [ ] Don't chase every trend people on X are hyping.
- [ ] Build a solution to a real problem you find in a field you know well, to show domain knowledge
- [ ] Build software for friends or a small business to show real-world impact
- [ ] Start a freelance practice or small startup around something you built
