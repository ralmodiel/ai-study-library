Find Hidden AI Engineering Jobs by Searching for Skills, Not Job Titles

Summary

Searching LinkedIn only for the title "AI engineer" misses many relevant roles. The creator searched for a skill (LangChain) instead of the job title and got many more results. He cites a hiring-platform analysis of over 45,000 postings: more than 53% of companies ask for skills that don't match the job type. His advice is to search for the tools and techniques you've used, then read the job descriptions to see whether the work matches.

Key points

  • On LinkedIn, the creator filtered for AI engineering jobs with under 10 applicants, posted in the past 24 hours, and noted the number of results.
  • The same search with 'LangChain' instead of 'AI engineer' returned many more results.
  • A hiring platform (not named) analyzed over 45,000 job postings and found that more than 53% of companies want skills that don't match the job type.
  • Job titles are unreliable, so you may be qualified for roles whose titles don't say 'AI engineer'.
  • Search for the skills and tools you've used, such as RAG, LangChain, agents, tool use, RAGAS and agent evals.
  • Read the job description, not just the title, to check whether the work matches what you've built.
  • Use filters (few applicants, posted in the last 24 hours) to find fresh, less crowded postings.

Try this

  • Search job boards for the specific tools and skills you've used (e.g., RAG, LangChain, agents, tool use, RAGAS, agent evals), not just for 'AI engineer'.
  • Read the full job descriptions to see whether the work matches your experience, even when the title doesn't.
  • On LinkedIn, filter for recent postings (past 24 hours) with few applicants (under 10).
  • Compare how many results a title search returns against a skill search.
