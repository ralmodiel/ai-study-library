Theo's YouTube Packaging Masterclass: Thumbnails, Titles and the Algorithm

Summary

Theo (t3.gg) explains how to package YouTube videos so the right viewers stop scrolling, click, and watch to the end. He covers how he makes thumbnails and titles (give a reason to click, keep text to a minimum, use relatable 'enemy' images like Jira), and explains that the YouTube algorithm recommends videos by comparing overlapping watch histories. His main strategy is to raise your floor so your worst videos do better, rather than chase one viral hit. This is about growing a developer audience and personal brand, not AI engineering.

Key points

  • Packaging comes down to three steps: get the right person to stop scrolling, get them to click, and make sure they enjoy watching to the end.
  • Repelling the wrong viewer matters too. Clicks from people the video isn't for cause early bounces, and YouTube reads those as clickbait and stops showing the video.
  • Think in reasons, not titles: the title should say why you'd click, not what the video is (e.g. 'Anthropic Fights Back' instead of 'Opus 4.8 is 5% better').
  • Keep thumbnail text to a minimum and use it as decoration. Show emblems, incentives or a relatable 'enemy' (like a messy Jira backlog) that makes viewers feel something.
  • Viewers forget the thumbnail about 30 seconds into the video, so it only needs to earn the click, not explain the content.
  • Theo describes the algorithm as diff-based. It recommends videos to people whose watch histories overlap with others who watched them, using roughly your last 500 watches. It no longer uses tags or categories.
  • A new video can get a head start if engaged viewers watch it fully, even while it's unlisted, so it lands in the right watch histories. Theo did this for Ethan Niser's first videos.
  • Raise your floor: aim for consistent minimum performance (e.g. ~10K views on your worst videos) to attract sponsors, instead of chasing big hits. Effort doesn't predict views: a 3-week video got ~50K while a 2-hour one got 200K.

Try this

  • Write titles that give a reason to click, not a description of the video.
  • Keep thumbnail text to a minimum. Show the topic's emblem or a relatable 'enemy' that makes viewers feel something.
  • Package each video so it repels viewers it isn't for, to avoid early bounces.
  • Repackage videos that underperform (new thumbnail and title) and compare the analytics.
  • Before going public, share a new video with engaged community members so it lands in the right watch histories.
  • Set a minimum bar each video has to hit, so your worst videos get better.
  • Learn to make thumbnails by hand before trying to automate it.
