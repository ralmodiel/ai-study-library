# Gemma 4: Google's Open Models for On-Device, Offline AI

## Summary

Melvin Vivas shares Google's promo video for Gemma 4 and asks whether Gemma might come to Apple devices. In the video, Google presents Gemma as a family of open models that can run on a phone without an internet connection, with sizes chosen for different devices and tasks. Developers in Africa, Thailand and Peru describe fine-tuning Gemma for local languages and for offline apps in places like schools, hospitals and maternal health care.

## Key points

- Most frontier models need an internet connection, which shuts out billions of people. Gemma's goal is strong AI that can run anywhere, on any device.
- Gemma is an open model family. Small sizes are built for mobile, and larger sizes focus on coding and agentic tasks.
- Gemma 4 builds on research done for Gemini 3. It is multimodal and can act as an agent directly on a phone, for example coding basic Android apps.
- For small models, the main goal ('North Star') is intelligence per byte of memory.
- Because the weights are open, you can download the model and fine-tune it, for example for a low-resource language like Quechua or for Thai.
- On-device models allow offline apps that use no mobile data and make no API calls. That suits schools, hospitals and remote areas such as the Andes.
- Example from the video: an offline app used every day to help lower maternal mortality.
- Google says open-source AI is key to making the technology available to everyone and to preserving cultures.
