Making a 40-Second Anime Episode Locally with ComfyUI on an RTX 3090

Summary

Melvin Vivas shows a short anime clip he made entirely on his own PC. Grok Bot acted as the "director" that coordinated the production, ComfyUI ran the generation workflows, and a single RTX 3090 did the work. Images came from Krea-2 Turbo (FP8) and video from MiniMax H3 (INT8) using Ref2VA. His point is that quantized open models now make it possible to produce multi-shot video on consumer hardware at no cost.

Key points

  • The setup has three roles: Grok Bot is the director that coordinates production, ComfyUI is the production studio that runs the workflows, and an RTX 3090 (24 GB) is the hardware.
  • Image generation used Krea-2 Turbo, quantized to FP8.
  • Video generation used MiniMax H3, quantized to INT8, in Ref2VA mode. Ref2VA appears to be reference-to-video, which starts from a reference image.
  • Quantization (FP8/INT8) is what lets large image and video models fit and run on a single 24 GB consumer GPU.
  • Running locally means there are no per-generation API costs. The creator says it's 'free' because it runs on his PC.
  • The finished result was a 40-second anime episode built from multiple generated clips.
  • The workflow is to have an LLM assistant plan the shots and prompts, generate keyframe images, then animate them into video clips.
