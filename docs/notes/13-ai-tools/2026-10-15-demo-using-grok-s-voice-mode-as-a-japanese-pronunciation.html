Demo: Using Grok's Voice Mode as a Japanese Pronunciation Coach

Summary

A short demo where Melvin Vivas asks Grok in voice mode to teach him basic Japanese words. Grok gives three everyday words, breaks each into syllables with stress tips, listens to him repeat them, and then has him say all three together. It shows a voice AI assistant working as an interactive language tutor. There's no engineering content in it, only a product use case.

Key points

  • Grok's voice mode can run a back-and-forth tutoring session: it teaches, waits for you to repeat, and gives feedback.
  • The words taught: ohayo (good morning), arigato (thank you), sumimasen (excuse me / sorry).
  • Grok splits each word into beats, e.g. su-mi-ma-sen is four beats with weight on the first 'su', and the 'ga' in arigato is soft.
  • The learner can steer the pace, e.g. asking to go 'one by one', and the assistant adapts.
  • After the single words, the assistant has the learner say them together, then offers a next lesson (self-introduction).
  • The creator claims voice assistants like this could replace apps like Duolingo. That's an opinion, not something the demo shows.
