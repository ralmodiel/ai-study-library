## Summary

Melvin Vivas reshares a Black Forest Labs announcement that director Martin Scorsese is now an advisor to the company. The 30-second clip shows him storyboarding a scene with the FLUX image model. He describes the setting, the mood and the camera position out loud, and FLUX turns that into storyboard frames. It's mainly industry news, but it gives a short example of prompting an image model the way a director would brief a production designer.

## Key points

- Black Forest Labs, the company behind the FLUX image models, has made Martin Scorsese an advisor. They say they want human taste and craft at the center of 'visual intelligence'.
- The clip shows a real storyboarding session in which FLUX produces frames from Scorsese's spoken scene descriptions.
- His prompt layers the setting ('a town, not a village, not a city'), the period and feel ('almost medieval', 'doesn't feel modern'), concrete details ('narrow cobblestone streets', 'twisting and turning main road') and camera direction ('put the camera higher looking down').
- Takeaway for prompting: state the scale, era, physical details and camera angle explicitly to get controllable cinematic images.
- Scorsese compares the result to Cecil B. DeMille having production designers make oil paintings of scenes, meaning AI frames can play the role of pre-visualization concept art.
- Trend signal: generative image labs are bringing in established creative figures to guide model taste and to work their way into film pre-production.
