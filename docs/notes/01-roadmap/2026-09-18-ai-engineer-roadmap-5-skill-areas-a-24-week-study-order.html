Summary

Bashiri Smith answers three questions people ask about becoming an AI engineer: what to study, in what order and for how long, and how to get and pass interviews. The plan rests on a GitHub repo of 4,894 real AI engineering job descriptions. It splits the skills into five areas and orders them practical-first over about 24 weeks. For the job search he says to apply to warm and cold leads instead of using easy apply, and to practise system design out loud.

Key points

  • The 5 skill areas, taken from 4,894 real AI engineering job descriptions: (1) LLMs & RAG, (2) integration, agents & orchestration, (3) ops & evaluation, (4) safety & ethics, (5) ML foundations.
  • About 70% of AI engineering roles are mostly LLMs and RAG. The other 30% also ask for one of the other four areas, and adding those on top of RAG puts you ahead of most applicants.
  • Study order and time: LLMs & RAG (5 weeks) → integration/agents/orchestration (6 weeks) → ops & evaluation (6 weeks) → safety & ethics (2 weeks) → ML foundations (5 weeks), about 24 weeks in total.
  • Learn the practical skills first and the model/ML theory second.
  • Ops and evaluation skills set you apart from 'fake AI engineers'. Safety and ethics is mostly conceptual: companies want to know you can build safe AI apps.
  • Job search loop: apply, track, convert. Don't spam easy apply. Tailor your resume for each role.
  • Split applications into warm leads (LinkedIn connections and friends at the company: nurture them and ask for a referral in a non-weird way) and cold leads (find a contact's name on LinkedIn, then look up their email with hunter.io).
  • Passing AI engineering interviews is mostly about system design, so practise it out loud with a real person.

Try this

  • Study the five areas in order: LLMs & RAG (5 wk), integration/agents/orchestration (6 wk), ops & evaluation (6 wk), safety & ethics (2 wk), ML foundations (5 wk).
  • Learn the practical building skills before the ML theory.
  • Stop spamming easy apply. Tailor your resume for each application and track your applications.
  • Split target companies into warm leads and cold leads.
  • For warm leads, nurture your connections and ask for a referral in a natural way.
  • For cold leads, find a contact on LinkedIn and get their email with hunter.io.
  • Practise system design interviews out loud with a real person.
