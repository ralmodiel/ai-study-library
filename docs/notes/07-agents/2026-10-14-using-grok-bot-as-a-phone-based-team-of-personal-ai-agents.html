Using Grok Bot as a Phone-Based Team of Personal AI Agents

Summary

Melvin Vivas describes a few days of using Grok Bot, an AI assistant that lets him run several specialised bots (content, tracking his X posts, coding/research) and control them from his phone or computer. His argument is that agents are moving from answering questions to doing work, which makes them useful for non-developers such as small-business owners and content creators. He also warns that people still need to review what the agents do, especially when they can see sensitive data or act on your behalf. This is an opinion and experience piece with no technical detail.

Key points

  • Multi-bot setup: give each bot one job (content help, tracking how your X posts perform, coding, research) and switch between them. It feels like managing a small team of AI co-workers.
  • Mobile-first workflow: send instructions from your phone, let a bot work, check the results, then give it the next task. You don't need to be at a desk.
  • You can start a conversation on your computer and pick it up on your phone with no remote desktop or complicated setup.
  • Example use: when a post starts getting attention (e.g., a repost by Elon Musk), ask your tracking bot what is happening and what people are reacting to.
  • Uses for non-coders: small businesses can research competitors, prepare reports, organise information and track follow-ups. Content creators can do research, content planning and performance analysis.
  • The main skill is knowing what you want to get done, not knowing how to code.
  • Caveat: these systems still make mistakes. Review their output, especially when they have access to sensitive information or can take actions for you.
  • Software development still needs a full computer, according to the creator.
  • Note: the transcript also writes the product as 'Rockbot', and the video gives no link or official source for 'Grok Bot', so check which product this actually is before relying on these notes.
