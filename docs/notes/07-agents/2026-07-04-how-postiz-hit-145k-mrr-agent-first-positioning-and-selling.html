How Postiz Hit $145K MRR: Agent-First Positioning and Selling Outcomes

This note summarises a video post by Melvin Vivas on X, posted 4 July 2026. The original belongs to Melvin Vivas.

Summary

Nevo David, founder of the open-source social media scheduler Postiz, explains why Postiz grows by about $1K MRR a day while many competitors stay under $1K–3K MRR. He credits four things: being clearly different instead of copying solved markets, targeting developers through open source, rebranding as "social media scheduling for agents" (with a CLI and an MCP server), and marketing the outcome (making money, going viral) rather than features. It is mostly founder and go-to-market advice, but it shows how building agent and MCP integrations into a product changes how it is positioned.

Key points

  • Start with the question 'What problem do you solve?' Social media scheduling was solved 15 years ago by Hootsuite and Buffer, so copying them leads to high churn and stalled MRR.
  • Features don't set you apart: being able to add 10 LinkedIn comments instead of 1 won't win customers. Find a 'blue ocean' inside the red ocean of competitors.
  • Postiz stood out with about 30 channels where competitors have about 10, including Web3, Telegram, Discord and Slack, which few tools served.
  • It targets technical and semi-technical users instead of small businesses and agencies. The open-source repo has about 30K stars, and developers self-host it, talk about it and build it into company workflows even when they don't pay.
  • It moved from n8n automations to agents with a CLI and an MCP server. The key step was changing the marketing message to 'a social media scheduling tool for agents', not just adding an agent feature.
  • Manual scheduling causes churn: users who must create posts every day lose motivation. At 3K MRR the founder was losing about 30% of customers a month. Agents can take a content source and schedule a whole month automatically.
  • Sell the outcome, not the tool. Learn from creators who already use the product how they make money (for example AI-generated UGC posted to social media), then package that as a repeatable playbook.
  • Focus on social media over SEO and ads because it builds a brand and word of mouth. Agents are a hot topic, and creator posts about them often pass 100K views.

Try this

  • Write down exactly what problem your product solves and check whether incumbents already solved it.
  • Look for an underserved audience or niche (for example developers) rather than competing on features.
  • Make agents part of your core marketing message (for example 'X for agents'), not just a feature buried in the product.
  • Offer a CLI and an MCP server so agents can use your product.
  • Interview existing users about how they make money with your tool and turn that into repeatable playbook content.
  • Put marketing effort into social media and creator collaborations to build a brand, rather than relying only on SEO and ads.
  • Add an MCP server and CLI to an existing app so AI agents can drive it, for example scheduling a month of social posts from one content source.
  • Build an agent that turns a content source (such as a blog or video) into social media posts and schedules them across platforms through the Postiz API or MCP.
