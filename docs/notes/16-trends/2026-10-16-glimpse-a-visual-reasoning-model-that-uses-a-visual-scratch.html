Glimpse: A Visual Reasoning Model That Uses a Visual Scratch Space

Summary

Melvin Vivas shares a launch video for Glimpse, the first visual reasoning ("visual thinking") model from Illurion AI. It is presented by co-founder and CEO Andrew Dye, who previously spent 12 years at Google Brain and Google DeepMind. The video explains how the model reasons about the physical world: it uses a "visual scratch space" that feeds crops, selected frames and new views back into its reasoning. It also gives claimed benchmark, cost and compute numbers.

Key points

  • Glimpse is pitched as the first in a new category of vision-specialized models that reason about the physical world from what they see.
  • Abilities these models need: following objects as they move and as the viewpoint changes, understanding how parts fit together, and linking an observation to what happens next.
  • Core technique: a 'visual scratch space'. Reasoning steps are conditioned on transformed images (crops, selected frames, new views) that are brought back into the reasoning loop.
  • Claimed results: frontier-level performance, competitive with 'Astra', on benchmarks covering counting, scientific chart reasoning, and video and robotic captioning.
  • Claimed cost: about 1/16 of Astra's token cost.
  • Built by a small team using only a few hundred GPUs.
  • Intended uses: robotics, science, engineering, and other work that needs physical-world understanding.
  • All performance numbers are the vendor's own launch claims and have not been checked independently.
