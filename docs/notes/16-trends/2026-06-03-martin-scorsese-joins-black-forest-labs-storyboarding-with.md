## Summary

Melvin Vivas reposts Black Forest Labs' announcement that director Martin Scorsese is now an advisor to the company. The 30-second clip shows a storyboarding session in which Scorsese describes a scene out loud and FLUX, Black Forest Labs' image model, generates frames from it. It is mostly industry news. The one practical lesson is how a director phrases scene prompts: setting, mood, specific details and camera angle.

## Key points

- Black Forest Labs, the maker of the FLUX image models, has named Martin Scorsese as an advisor. They say they want human taste and craft at the center of 'visual intelligence'.
- Use case shown: using a text-to-image model (FLUX) to make storyboard frames for film pre-production.
- Scorsese's prompt pattern: give the setting precisely ('a town, not a village, not a city'), then the mood ('doesn't feel modern', 'almost medieval'), then concrete details ('narrow cobblestone streets', 'twisting main road'), then the camera direction ('put the camera higher, looking down').
- Scorsese compares AI storyboards to Cecil B. DeMille having production designers paint oil paintings of scenes.
- Trend: generative image companies are bringing in well-known creative professionals as advisors to shape their products.
