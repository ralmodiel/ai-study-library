Is Indie Hacking Dead? How AI Coding Changes the Micro-SaaS Playbook

This note summarises a video post by Melvin Vivas on X, posted 30 July 2026. The original belongs to Melvin Vivas.

Summary

Melvin Vivas shares a Fireship clip. It argues that AI coding tools have taken away the old indie-hacker advantage, which was simply being able to write code. Now anyone with a cheap AI subscription can build their own software or copy your idea, so a simple micro-SaaS is much harder to sell. Vivas suggests looking at opportunities around AI agents instead. The clip says people who are good at distribution, branding or owning proprietary data can still win.

Key points

  • The classic indie-hacking playbook from the last decade: learn to code, find a niche, ship a micro-SaaS (e.g. Next.js + Stripe), build in public, and repeat until something makes money.
  • Pieter Levels (levelsio) is the 'patron saint' of the movement. He has shipped several profitable apps, each from a single PHP file.
  • The old moat was coding itself, because it was hard and expensive and few people could execute well.
  • 'Ideas are worthless, execution is everything' matters less now that execution costs about $20/month in AI subscriptions.
  • Users may build their own personal software instead of paying $29/month for a SaaS. The clip's example is Claude Opus 5 building a better version in about 20 minutes.
  • Building in public is riskier because AI-assisted copycats can quickly clone ideas and roadmaps posted on Twitter/X.
  • AI raises the ceiling (more productivity) but crowds the floor (more competitors), and both trends grow as AI gets cheaper and smarter.
  • What still wins: distribution, branding, and getting proprietary data. Melvin's own take is to focus on something around AI agents.

Try this

  • Don't count on coding skill alone as your moat for a SaaS product.
  • Work on distribution, branding, or getting proprietary data to stay competitive.
  • Look for product opportunities around AI agents instead of simple micro-SaaS tools.
  • Think about the copycat risk before posting detailed ideas and roadmaps in public.
  • Build a product around AI agents instead of a simple micro-SaaS (Melvin's suggestion).
