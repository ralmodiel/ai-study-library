Summary

Melvin Vivas reacts to a quoted post showing the data-center infrastructure used to train the Muse foundation models, including Muse Wonder. The quoted post says the models were trained at Prometheus, a gigawatt-scale supercluster in New Albany, Ohio, with hundreds of thousands of GPUs. Its main point is that at this scale, a single underperforming host can slow down an entire training run. There is no speech in the 24-second video; it shows the hardware and cabling.

Key points

  • The Muse foundation models, including Muse Wonder, were trained on the Prometheus supercluster.
  • Prometheus is described as gigawatt-scale and located in New Albany, Ohio.
  • The cluster has hundreds of thousands of GPUs.
  • In large synchronous training runs, one slow or underperforming host can hold back the whole job (the straggler problem), so detecting weak hosts is a core infrastructure concern.
  • Frontier model training now runs at gigawatt-level power, which shows how much compute and energy foundation models need.
  • The post is a short reaction with little technical detail; the quoted post is cut off before explaining how Prometheus was engineered.
