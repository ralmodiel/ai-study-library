In the quoted post, Tavus says its goal is to make talking to a machine feel like talking to a friend or coworker, with small human cues like facial expressions, shifting in a chair and knowing when to speak. There's no tutorial here. It's a quick look at the state of the art in conversational video agents.

## Key points

- Tavus builds real-time AI video avatars you can have a spoken conversation with.
- Their stated goal: talking to a machine should feel as natural as talking to a friend or coworker.
- Making it feel human depends on small cues: facial expressions, body movement (like shifting in a chair) and turn-taking (knowing when to speak).
- In the demo the AI keeps track of details across turns: the date (the 26th), the restaurant choice, the headcount going from 12 to 13 when Marcus is added, and the lunch time moving to 12:30.
- It also handles small calculations and planning in conversation, e.g. $20 per person × 13 = $260 for a group gift.
- Trend to note: multimodal, low-latency voice-and-video agents are getting close to human-like conversation.
