Is AI Getting Cheaper? How to Measure LLM Cost Per Task and Per Intelligence Level

This note summarises a video post by Melvin Vivas on X, posted 14 October 2026. The original belongs to Melvin Vivas.

Summary

Melvin Vivas shares a video by Theo (t3.gg). Theo argues that AI is getting cheaper even though AI bills keep rising, and that price per token is the wrong way to measure it. He breaks cost into three parts: price per token, tokens used per task, and cost to reach a given level of intelligence. Using Artificial Analysis data, he shows that the cost of a fixed intelligence level drops by far more than 10x a year. Bills go up because people use models much more (Jevons paradox), not because intelligence got more expensive.

Key points

  • Price per token alone is misleading. Look at three things: (1) price per token, (2) tokens needed to finish a task, and (3) cost to reach a given intelligence level.
  • Top models did get pricier per token: GPT-5 medium was $1.25 in / $10 out per million tokens, while GPT-6 Astra is $10 in / $50 out. Cheap models also improved, though: GLM 5.3 Flash is about $0.15 in / $0.50 out and beats GPT-5 at agentic work.
  • Token-hungry models eat up their price advantage. Gemini 3.8 Flash is more than 10x cheaper per token than Astra, but its cost per task is less than 3x cheaper. Sonnet 5 cost more per task than Astra because it burns tokens.
  • Pick models by workload. For jobs heavy on input (for example, categorizing 800K tokens), choose the cheapest input-token price. For agentic coding, caching, reliability and token efficiency matter more.
  • Compare models with a cost-vs-intelligence chart and its Pareto frontier. Going from a score of about 45 ($0.82 per task) to the 50s ($3.26) triples the cost for a few points.
  • Hold intelligence fixed and look at cost over time. DeepSeek V4 Flash roughly matches GPT-5 for about 20x less. o1 Preview's level cost about $15 per task in Sept 2024, and Grok 3 Mini beat it for about $0.16 by Feb 2025. Each price drop comes faster than the last.
  • Bills rise because of Jevons paradox: cheaper, more capable models run longer, end-to-end agentic tasks, so total usage and spend go up.
  • Small models with good tool use (search, tool calls, long-running agentic work) can beat last year's frontier models, even when they hold less built-in knowledge.

Try this

  • Compare models by cost per task and cost per intelligence level, not just price per token.
  • Use Artificial Analysis cost-vs-intelligence charts and the Pareto frontier to find the cheapest model that is good enough for your task.
  • Pick models by workload: lowest input-token price for jobs that ingest a lot of text, and token efficiency, caching and reliability for agentic coding.
  • Before defaulting to a frontier model, try cheap capable models like GLM 5.3 Flash or DeepSeek V4 Flash and give them good tools such as search.
  • Set up agent-managed infrastructure: let a coding agent (Claude Code or Codex) create production, staging and per-PR environments through a platform MCP server such as Railway's.
  • Build a simple cost tracker that logs tokens and cost per task across models, to find your own price-performance sweet spot.
