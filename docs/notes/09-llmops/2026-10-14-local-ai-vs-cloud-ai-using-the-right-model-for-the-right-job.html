Local AI vs Cloud AI: Using the Right Model for the Right Job

Summary

Melvin Vivas explains when running AI models locally (on your own computer or company infrastructure) makes sense compared with cloud AI APIs. He covers three reasons to go local: privacy and control, cost at high volume, and working offline. He also argues that small fine-tuned models can match or beat large general-purpose models on narrow tasks. His recommendation is a hybrid strategy: local models for repetitive, high-volume or privacy-sensitive work, and cloud models for advanced tasks like coding.

Key points

  • Privacy and control: hospitals, banks and law firms can process sensitive documents on their own infrastructure without sending data to an outside AI provider. They still need proper security controls.
  • Cost: with high volume (thousands of invoices, emails or documents a day), running a smaller model on your own hardware can be cheaper than paying for every API request.
  • Offline AI: local models work without internet, which helps mobile apps, factories, remote sites and places with poor connectivity.
  • You don't always need a huge LLM. Tasks like classifying emails, pulling data from documents or detecting sensitive information can often be done by a small specialized model, sometimes on ordinary computers without expensive GPUs.
  • A small model fine-tuned on your company's own data (support tickets, email types, industry documents) can sometimes outperform a much larger general model on that one task.
  • Example: in a post on X, Coinbase described fine-tuning a small Qwen-based model for fraud management. Smaller models are cheaper and faster to run locally.
  • Cloud AI is easier to use, needs less maintenance and gives access to more powerful models. At low request volumes, an API may actually be cheaper.
  • Recommended hybrid: use local models for repetitive, high-volume or privacy-sensitive tasks, and cloud models for advanced capabilities like coding when your data policies allow it.

Try this

  • Sort your AI workloads by volume, privacy sensitivity and difficulty to decide what should run locally and what should use the cloud.
  • For narrow tasks such as classification, extraction or detecting sensitive data, try a small specialized model before reaching for a large general-purpose LLM.
  • Think about fine-tuning a small model (for example, a Qwen-based one) on your own company data for specific tasks.
  • Compare API cost per request with the cost of running your own hardware at your real request volume.
  • Fine-tune a small local model to classify incoming emails or support tickets.
  • Build a local, offline pipeline that extracts fields from invoices or industry-specific documents.
  • Run a local model that detects sensitive information in documents before anything is sent to a cloud API.
  • Build a hybrid router that sends high-volume or sensitive tasks to a local model and complex tasks (like coding) to a cloud model.
