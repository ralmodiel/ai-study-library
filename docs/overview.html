Free guides they shared

  • 5 books to upgrade from software engineer into Ai/ML engineer, 1 pages: A one-page reading list of five books for software engineers moving into AI or ML engineering. It covers math (Mathematics for Machine Learning), classic ML (Hands-On ML), deep learning (Goodfellow et al.), building AI products (Chip Huyen's AI Engineering) and LLMOps (Abi Aryan). Each book comes with a short note on whether it is essential for AI engineers, ML engineers or both. The page ends with a pitch for the creator's paid Skool community.
  • Agentic design patterns: The AI engineer's field guide to designing, building, and deploying agents (2026 edition), 48 pages: Bashiri Smith (BASWE) wrote this 48-page field guide for software engineers moving into AI engineering. It covers what an agent is, five core patterns (ReAct, planning, reflection, routing and supervisor-worker), 20 more workflow, reasoning, coordination, control and knowledge patterns, and how to design agents (context engineering, tool design, state, MCP/A2A and frameworks). It then covers evaluating agents (eval stack, LLM-as-judge, metrics, tracing) and deploying them (runtime, reliability, security, cost, rollout). It ends with a reference architecture, a production checklist, 12 interview questions and five portfolio projects. Read Parts 1–2 in order, do each build exercise, or pick one of the three reading paths (one evening, one week, one month).
  • Evaluation Field Guide (baswe.ai engineer accelerator – Ops and Evaluation module), 41 pages: Bashiri Smith's 41-page practitioner's guide to evaluating AI systems. It covers eval fundamentals (eval-driven development, golden datasets, metric types, basic statistics), deterministic and overlap metrics for LLM outputs, LLM-as-a-judge (rubric design, judge biases, checking judges against human labels), RAG evaluation (retrieval metrics and the RAG triad), agent trajectory evaluation, model benchmarking and fine-tune evaluation, and production evals (tracing, online signals, A/B tests, drift, guardrails, CI eval gates). It ends with a map of the eval tool stack, four portfolio projects and interview signals. Read it once end to end, then use it as a reference, and build one of the portfolio projects.
  • How to become an expert in RAG (BASWE AI Engineer Field Guide), 1 pages: A one-page field guide from BASWE that lays out a 5-stage path to mastering retrieval-augmented generation. The stages are: get the mental model, build a base pipeline (chunking, embeddings, vector DB, retrieval), upgrade retrieval (hybrid search, reranking, RAG-Fusion, HyDE), ship it to production (evals, latency/cost, guardrails, monitoring), and explore advanced methods (agentic, knowledge-graph and multimodal RAG, RAPTOR, ColBERT, corrective RAG). It lists free courses and YouTube videos for each step and ends with a pitch for the creator's paid Skool community. Treat the stages as a checklist and, as the guide says, ship one real project at every stage.
  • System Card: Claude Opus 4.8, 244 pages: Anthropic's 244-page system card for Claude Opus 4.8 (May 28, 2026). It reports pre-deployment evaluations covering Responsible Scaling Policy risks (chemical/biological weapons, AI R&D, misalignment), cyber capabilities, safeguards and harmlessness, agentic safety (including the first live prompt-injection bug bounty), alignment, model welfare and capability benchmarks. Read it as a detailed case study in how a frontier lab evaluates a model: red-teaming, behavioral audits, evaluation awareness and chain-of-thought monitorability, and benchmarks like SWE-bench, OSWorld and MCP Atlas.
  • The AI Pivot Field Guide, 23 pages: A 2026 guide from the baswe.Ai Engineer Accelerator with three routes into AI engineering: mid-level software engineers (9 to 12 months), new grads (12 months) and non-technical domain experts who want to build agents (9 months). Its main idea is to build first. Start with LLM APIs and RAG, then agents, ops and evaluation, then safety, and leave ML math and foundations for last. It also covers job-market and salary data, portfolio and interview advice, and ends with a resource library of free and free-tier links. Pick your chapter, follow its steps in order, and use each step's 'Start here' links.
  • The Interview Engine: The 7-Step System to Land AI Engineering Interviews, 12 pages: A BASWE field guide that lays out a 7-step job-search system for software engineers going after AI engineering roles. The steps are a one-page ATS-friendly resume, tailoring the resume to each job with a Claude prompt, exporting your LinkedIn connections, a three-tab job tracker, using Claude to match connections to companies for referrals, finding hiring managers' emails with Hunter.io and Apollo, and a cold email template with a follow-up schedule. It includes copy-paste prompts, message templates and a weekly routine aimed at an 8–12% application-to-interview rate. Work through it step by step, then repeat the weekly loop.
